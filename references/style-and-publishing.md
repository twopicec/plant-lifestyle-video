# Style and Publishing

Read this reference for covers, visual identity, titles, body copy, hashtags, or series positioning.

## Home-and-plant positioning

The channel identity is not “plant encyclopedia.” It is a lived-in home where plants create atmosphere, small domestic problems, useful routines, and stories. Let the plant remain visually desirable while showing the mess, awkwardness, surprise, or maintenance that makes the video human. This route should be compatible with future home, storage, tools, pots, furniture, and lifestyle partnerships without turning the video into an advertisement.

## Cover identity

Before designing, search the current project and `Desktop/视频创作/素材` for earlier files named `封面` or `新封面` and inspect them as style references.

Recurring visual language:

- 3:4 portrait cover for Xiaohongshu.
- Flat pale mint or light green background, sometimes with one low-contrast circle.
- One real plant or object cutout as the main subject, with a thick white sticker edge and yellow offset shadow.
- Large, condensed Chinese title in golden yellow or orange with a very thick dark forest-green outline.
- Strong top-first hierarchy: a smaller setup line, one oversized result line, and at most one tilted micro-label.
- High contrast and hand-cut collage energy; avoid elegant magazine layouts, gradients, dense decorations, and tiny copy.

The cover should reveal the topic but preserve curiosity. Use the strongest physical evidence, not a generic finished-plant portrait. Keep every important leaf tip, root edge, and text block inside a safe margin. For image edits, preserve requested invariants such as the original root ball and use non-destructive version names.

## Cover copy

Use two beats:

- Setup: what the creator thought they were doing.
- Result: the unexpected thing that happened.

Keep the result line short enough to read as a thumbnail. A small label may intensify the evidence, but it should not repeat the entire title. Do not add unsupported numbers or sensational claims.

## Publishing title

The post title should complement rather than merely repeat the cover. Favor one clear surprise plus the subject:

- action + unexpected result;
- normal expectation + visual contradiction;
- specific domestic inconvenience + reveal.

Avoid keyword piles, false urgency, and generic phrases such as “一定要看” or “后悔没早知道.”

## Body copy

Write in the creator's spoken voice:

1. Repeat the most relatable moment in one or two short lines.
2. Give the minimum backstory.
3. Explain the trigger and reveal.
4. Add two or three practical takeaways in plain language.
5. Close with a genuine opinion or next-topic bridge.

Paragraphs should be short and easy to scan. Useful information may expand slightly beyond the voiceover, but it must remain accurate and consistent with the footage. Do not turn the caption into a formal care guide.

Use a small, relevant hashtag set: plant name, action, plant care, home greenery, and creator/lifestyle category. Avoid long walls of tags.

## Series continuity

Use next-episode teasers only when the next topic is already meaningful. A good teaser answers a new question created by the current video, such as why an ordinary plant is unexpectedly fun, how it changes a room, or what happens after propagation. Keep the current episode complete on its own.
