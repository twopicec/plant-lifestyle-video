# Plant Lifestyle Video

Turn existing plant footage into a lived-in home-and-plant story that is entertaining first and useful along the way. Favor the creator's recognizable lifestyle route over a formal horticulture tutorial.

## Working approach

1. Inspect the actual media before proposing edits. Locate the source clips, read metadata, sample the timeline, and use the recorded audio as the source of truth.
2. Match the requested stage. Review or diagnose without mutating files; when asked to build or change, implement and verify the deliverable.
3. Preserve originals. Create clearly named versions and never overwrite source footage, recordings, subtitles, covers, or prior approved outputs unless explicitly requested.
4. Ask only about choices that materially change the result. Infer file locations, timings, and existing style from the workspace whenever possible.
5. If the user asks to inspect music, sound effects, flower text, or a cover before finalizing, provide a review version or cue sheet first and wait for approval.

## Editorial spine

Avoid a chronological activity log. Find one contrast, surprise, problem, or reveal and build the video around it:

- Open directly on the strongest visual or result within the first 3–7 seconds. No greeting or logo intro unless requested.
- Give only the context needed to understand why the action matters.
- Let attempts, small failures, tactile sounds, and reactions create movement.
- Hold the clearest reveal at normal speed; speed up repetitive actions.
- Leave the viewer with two or three practical judgments, not a lecture.
- End on a relaxed observation, result, or genuinely connected next-episode idea. Do not use generic engagement bait.

Do not invent missing shots or imply that a shot exists. Rebuild the story from available footage and acknowledge constraints such as no before-shot or no watering shot.

## Voice and writing identity

- Write Mandarin that sounds spoken at home: concrete, lightly self-deprecating, and a little playful.
- Prefer short observations such as “好家伙” or a specific domestic inconvenience over polished presenter language.
- Avoid scenic-introduction prose, motivational filler, exaggerated influencer urgency, and repetitive rhetorical questions.
- Add usefulness through timely judgments: what signal matters, what not to overreact to, and what simple choice prevents a common mistake.
- Keep the creator's wording when supplied. Edit for breath, rhythm, and clarity without replacing their personality.

## Local conventions

- Primary platform is Xiaohongshu, with pacing that also works on Douyin.
- Default video shape is vertical; preserve the source frame rate and exact approved timing unless a retime is requested.
- The established music and sound-effects library is `/Library/Audio/Apple Loops/Apple/Final Cut Pro Sound Effects/`.
- When a playful game-like sound direction is requested, prefer the `星露谷` subfolder and keep all added effects from that folder for a coherent palette.
- If the creator plans to add music themselves, do not bake music into the output. Deliver the aligned sound-effects track separately when useful.

## Completion standard

Before handoff, verify duration, dimensions, frame rate, audio sample rate, audible speech, peak safety, subtitle timing, readable cover text, and that the requested output excludes anything the user chose to add themselves. Make the result easy to review with a direct local file link and a concise note about any evidence-based timing correction.
